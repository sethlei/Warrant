# Case 01 — GW150914: the first direct gravitational-wave detection (physics)

**Paper (cite-only):** *Observation of Gravitational Waves from a Binary Black Hole Merger.*
B. P. Abbott et al. (LIGO Scientific Collaboration & Virgo Collaboration), 2016.
**Phys. Rev. Lett. 116, 061102.** DOI [`10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.061102`](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.061102)
· arXiv:1602.03837 · **CC-BY 3.0**.
**Source a reader can pull:** the public strain data at the Gravitational Wave Open Science Center
(gwosc.org), DOI [`10.7935/K5MW2F23`](https://doi.org/10.7935/K5MW2F23); a published step-by-step
reproduction exists (Brown et al., *Reproducing GW150914*, arXiv:2010.07244).

> This case ships the DAG analysis and cites the paper; it reproduces none of the paper's text.

## The load-bearing claim

*"A transient gravitational-wave signal (GW150914) was observed at >5σ significance (false-alarm
rate < 1 per 203,000 yr), produced by the merger of two black holes of ≈36 and ≈29 M☉."*

This is a textbook case for the method because the **single sentence welds together three claims of
very different soundness** — and the popular reading ("they detected two black holes colliding")
collapses them into one. The graph pulls them apart.

## The verification graph  `root = AND(L1, L2, L3)`

| leaf | sub-claim | cell `(s_pass,s_death)` | checker | a reader can… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | the event is not detector noise (the >5σ / FAR claim) | **SOUND `(H,H)`** | a **permutation-null**: time-slide the two detectors' data by non-physical offsets to build the empirical noise background; the event sits far beyond it | re-run the time-slide background on the public strain data |
| **L2** | the same waveform appears in **both** detectors with the right ~7 ms inter-detector delay | **WITNESS `(H,L)`** | exhibit the coincident, time-shifted waveform in Hanford + Livingston | pull both detectors' data and overlay them |
| **L3** | the source is a **binary black-hole merger of ≈36+29 M☉** | **FUZZY/PARTIAL `(L,L)`–`(L,H)`** | matched-filtering against general-relativity waveform templates + Bayesian parameter estimation; a good fit does not exclude every alternative, and masses/spins carry posterior width | inspect the posteriors; a gross template-mismatch *would* refute, a fit does not certify |

**Compose.** L1 is `(H,H)` and **passes** — the detection itself is sound, certified by a
permutation argument a reader can re-run. L2 is a `(H,L)` **witness** and passes. L3 is the
soft leaf: the *astrophysics* (it was two black holes, of these masses) is model-dependent
judgment, not a sound certification. The AND's pass-strength is therefore **capped at L3's
soundness** — the weakest link.

## The vector read-out

```
VERDICT: PASS  (the claim holds), but pass-strength is CAPPED by L3
  SOUND-realized      : {L1}      ← "a gravitational wave was detected" is proven (permutation-null)
  WITNESS-realized    : {L2}      ← present in both detectors, coincident
  FUZZY residual (to-do): {L3}    ← "from a 36+29 M☉ binary black hole" is the model-dependent judgment
```

## What the method surfaces

The headline is not wrong — it is *two claims of different kinds wearing one sentence*. **"A
gravitational wave was detected"** is among the most soundly-established results in modern physics,
because its significance rests on a permutation-null, not on a confident fit. **"It came from two
black holes of these masses"** is a strong, careful, but *model-dependent* inference — exactly the
FUZZY leaf the method routes up the disprove ladder and never silently promotes to SOUND. A reader
can re-run L1 on public data and watch the sound part be sound; the method's gift is making the
boundary between the proven part and the judgment part explicit instead of letting the certainty of
L1 bleed onto L3.
